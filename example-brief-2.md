# Example brief 2: the same format, four different jobs

> **This is a sampler, not a real morning.** [`example-brief.md`](example-brief.md)
> is an actual run from a working telecom setup, with live links you can open.
> This file is the opposite: every item below is an **invented illustration**,
> every link points at `example.com`, and no item names a real company doing a
> real thing. Nothing here is reporting and none of it should be repeated.
>
> It exists to answer one question: **does this format survive leaving telecom?**
> Each section is a beat belonging to a different person in a different field.
> Read them for shape, length, and the way each "why it matters" clause lands on
> a decision the reader actually makes.
>
> Your own brief would carry two to five beats from **one** field, not four
> unrelated ones. This file mixes them on purpose so you can see the range.

---

## Commercial real estate, capital markets beat

*Written for an analyst moving toward acquisitions.*

**[Lenders reprice a major asset class as spreads widen past a two-year high](https://example.com/cre-spreads)** - Several large balance-sheet lenders moved quoted spreads up by 40 to 60 basis points on one property type this week and pulled back from two secondary markets entirely. If you are underwriting anything in that class, your exit cap and your refinancing assumption are both now stale, and the deals still clearing are doing it with more equity rather than at the old basis.

**[A state legislature passes by-right conversion with a hard sunset date](https://example.com/conversion-bill)** - The bill removes discretionary review for office-to-residential conversions in qualifying buildings, but the entitlement window closes in thirty months. That turns a "someday" conversion thesis into a dated one: anything not filed before the sunset reverts to the old approval path, which historically ran eighteen months longer.

## Data centers, power and siting beat

*Written for a development manager who owns site selection.*

**[A regional grid operator tells its regulator the large-load queue now runs past 2031](https://example.com/queue-filing)** - The filing puts new interconnection study completion three to four years out in the operator's two most requested zones, and proposes a minimum-take tariff for loads above a threshold. Any site in those zones can no longer support a delivery commitment inside the decade, and sites with existing queue positions just became materially more valuable than raw land.

**[Switchgear lead times stretch again as a second manufacturer stops quoting firm dates](https://example.com/switchgear)** - Quoted lead times moved from roughly 90 weeks to beyond 120, and one supplier will now only give a window rather than a date. Electrical gear, not construction, is the critical path on most campuses at this point, so procurement has to start before the site is fully entitled or the schedule is fiction.

## Manufacturing and supply chain, cost beat

*Written for an estimator who defends bids.*

**[A trade determination lands on an input material with a 60-day effective date](https://example.com/trade-action)** - The preliminary duty applies to a category used in structural and enclosure work, and importers must post deposits from the effective date. Anything you bid at today's landed cost and deliver after that date carries the duty unless the contract has an escalation clause, so this is a two-month window to reprice open proposals.

**[A producer price index for a core input posts its largest monthly move in three years](https://example.com/ppi)** - The series moved enough that contingency built on last quarter's assumptions no longer covers it. Worth pulling into your next estimate review specifically, because the index is public and dated, which makes it a defensible basis for an escalation conversation with a client rather than a feeling that things cost more.

## Professional development, cross-field

*This beat looks similar in every industry, which is the point.*

**[A certifying body publishes a revised exam blueprint with a six-month transition](https://example.com/exam-blueprint)** - The revision reweights two domains and adds one, and candidates testing after the transition date sit the new version. If you were planning to test next year, sitting before the cutover means studying the blueprint you already started, which is usually the cheaper path.

**[A salary survey shows the premium for one credential narrowing while another widens](https://example.com/salary-survey)** - The gap between certified and non-certified compensation moved in opposite directions for two adjacent credentials in the same field. Useful mainly as an argument: if you are asking an employer to fund a certification, a dated third-party survey is a stronger case than an assertion that it is worth it.

---

**What to notice, if you are deciding whether this is for you:**

Every item names a **consequence for the reader**, not just an event. None of
them stop at "this happened." The beat files are where that discipline comes
from, and rewriting them for your work is the whole of `/setup`.
